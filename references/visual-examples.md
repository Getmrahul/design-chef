# Visual examples

Rahul supplied these screenshots as references for the skill. His [October 19, 2026 post](https://x.com/rahulmfg/status/2112230521417949192) identifies the portfolio, Kofe Flow, and Maaa as projects made with its earlier version, while noting that content density and layouts still needed improvement.

The notes below interpret the supplied screenshots. They are not a usability study or a verification of the live sites, product claims, accessibility, responsive behavior, or animation.

## How to use this collection

- Choose a reference for a specific principle that supports the current brief.
- Inspect the image itself and show it inline when discussing it with the user. These files are bundled locally; opening the live website is optional unless current behavior is needed.
- Borrow hierarchy, restraint, contrast, or the role of imagery. Do not automatically reuse the composition, copy, branding, artwork, or product claims.
- Explain both the useful choice and its tradeoff. A supplied reference is not blanket approval of every element.
- Do not make three layout options by merely reskinning these three sites. Develop alternatives around the actual product and task.

## 1. Rahul's portfolio — typography and whitespace

Source context: [rahulm.me](https://rahulm.me), identified in the original post.

![Rahul's portfolio: oversized sans-serif and italic serif headline, generous whitespace, small introduction, and blue paper plane](images/rahul-portfolio.png)

**What works as a reference**

- The headline establishes a clear reading order. Contrasting sans-serif and italic serif typography adds character without a complicated surrounding interface.
- Generous whitespace separates the introduction, main statement, supporting sentence, and work history.
- A single blue paper plane supplies a recognizable visual accent. Most of the page remains neutral.
- Navigation and career context stay visually quieter than the main message.

**What to reconsider for another task**

- This is a portfolio introduction, not a focused transactional screen. Its navigation and project browsing suit that purpose; a task screen may need a much more explicit next action.
- Large display type and wide empty areas need deliberate adaptation on small screens.
- The paper plane's suitability depends on the product story. A memorable object is not a reason to add unrelated decoration.

Use this reference for editorial hierarchy, typography-led identity, and restraint.

## 2. Maaa — a focused message with personality

Source context: [maaa.app](https://maaa.app), identified in the original post.

![Maaa: character peeking out of a notch, a centered emotional headline, short supporting copy, and Mac and Windows downloads](images/maaa.png)

**What works as a reference**

- One central promise and one short supporting line carry most of the explanation.
- The character in the notch connects personality to the product's placement and behavior.
- Download is the main intent. The two platform buttons are alternatives within that intent, demonstrating why “one primary action” does not mean “exactly one button.”
- Platform requirements sit close to their relevant buttons.

**What to reconsider for another task**

- The large gap above the headline can delay the main message or action on shorter viewports. Preserve breathing room without copying the gap literally.
- The Mac-focused wording alongside a Windows download may need clearer platform-specific explanation.
- The visible demo controls imply an interactive preview, but the screenshot cannot establish how it behaves. Verify playback controls, keyboard use, and reduced-motion behavior in an implementation.
- A character can explain a companion product; it is not appropriate for every product category.

Use this reference for concise messaging, a clear action group, and product-specific personality.

## 3. Kofe Flow — atmosphere and product context

Source context: [kofeflow.com](https://kofeflow.com), identified in the original post.

![Kofe Flow: dark forest background, large white headline, download links, a bright timer preview, and a lower proof strip](images/kofe-flow.png)

**What works as a reference**

- The dark background and bright product preview create a clear contrast between mood and the software being shown.
- The headline names both the user benefit and where the product lives.
- The product screenshot gives concrete context beyond a decorative illustration.
- The forest imagery establishes atmosphere through one cohesive visual treatment.

**What to reconsider for another task**

- This is the densest example: navigation, a category label, a large headline, a paragraph, two download badges, a demo link, reassurance copy, and a proof strip all appear together. It is useful for atmosphere and product presentation, not as the target density for a simple task.
- Consider shortening the supporting paragraph, making the relevant download route easier to identify, and moving secondary proof below the initial action when it competes for attention.
- Metrics, ratings, and featured-publication claims in a reference are not evidence for another product. Use only independently supported claims for the product being built.
- The embedded timer screenshot includes statistics and secondary controls. It does not establish that these belong on every timer's initial screen.
- Background imagery needs readability checks at real sizes; the screenshot alone does not establish accessible contrast.

Use this reference for atmosphere, strong contrast, and showing the real product, while applying the skill's density checks.

## Shared principles

The useful common ground is a clear headline, a deliberate visual hierarchy, restrained color roles, and one memorable visual idea. The particular font pair, mascot, forest, split layout, or number of controls remains a product-specific choice.

These desktop screenshots are a visual starting point. Validate mobile layouts, interactions, and accessibility separately.
